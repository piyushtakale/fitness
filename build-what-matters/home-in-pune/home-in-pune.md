


# Pune / PCMC Family Home Plan

> **Mission:** Buy a legal, liveable 2-BHK in Pune or PCMC that gives my parents and family long-term stability, while protecting my savings, career flexibility, and peace of mind.
>
> **Decision rule:** I will not buy because I feel rushed. I will buy only when the home, paperwork, loan, location, and total monthly cost all pass my checks.

---

## 1. Why this matters

This is not only a real-estate goal.

I want a stable home where my parents can live with comfort, dignity, and security. I want to be physically closer to my family in Maharashtra, take responsibility for the search rather than placing the full burden on them, and build a practical base for the future.

Pune / PCMC is meaningful because it can bring me closer to my parents in the Ulhasnagar–Thane area, my sister in Pune, and friends from school, diploma, and college. It may also give me a stronger sense of belonging while still offering a technology-career ecosystem.

A house is a long-term responsibility, not a deadline or a measure of my worth. Supporting my family also means staying healthy, emotionally present, professionally capable, and financially resilient.

### Money buckets

Keep money separated by purpose. Do not treat the entire investment corpus as one pool.

| Bucket | Job | Where it should sit conceptually |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly spending account | Rent, food, bills, transport, recurring spending | Bank account / working cash |
| Emergency fund | Job loss, family emergency, essential medical or relocation need | Liquid, low-volatility, easily accessible instruments |
| Home purchase fund | Down payment, stamp duty, registration, legal review, moving, setup | Lower-risk allocation as the purchase horizon approaches |
| Long-term investments | Retirement and long-term wealth building | Diversified long-term investments; do not raid casually for a near-term purchase |
| Family / opportunity buffer | Planned support, visits, sister’s needs, relocation or housing opportunity costs | Accessible, separately tracked money |

### Emergency-reserve target

Before a home purchase, hold at least **6 months of essential expenses** separately. Use the actual amount from the personal cash-flow sheet, not an arbitrary round number.

Essential expenses may include: rent or current housing, food, utilities, transport, insurance premiums, unavoidable family support, minimum debt payments, and medical needs.

### Home purchase costs to plan for

The flat price is not the full cost. Maintain a separate estimate for:

- Down payment.
- Stamp duty and registration.
- GST, if applicable to an under-construction property.
- Legal due diligence and documentation costs.
- Loan processing, valuation, technical verification, insurance, and bank charges.
- Brokerage, if any.
- Society deposits, parking, maintenance advance, utility setup.
- Basic furnishing, appliances, moving, repairs, grills/curtains/lighting, and unexpected work.

**Rule:** Do not use every rupee for the down payment. Retain the emergency reserve after all purchase-related costs.

## 5. Rough affordability model

These are planning examples, not loan quotes. Obtain current offers from multiple lenders before deciding.

### Example purchase structure

| Property price | 20% down payment | Illustrative loan | Notes |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| ₹70 lakh | ₹14 lakh | ₹56 lakh | Transaction and setup costs are extra |
| ₹75 lakh | ₹15 lakh | ₹60 lakh | Transaction and setup costs are extra |
| ₹80 lakh | ₹16 lakh | ₹64 lakh | Transaction and setup costs are extra |

At an illustrative 8.85% rate, a 20-year loan of ₹60 lakh is roughly ₹53,000 per month; a ₹64 lakh loan is roughly ₹56,500 per month. A longer tenure reduces EMI but increases total interest. Rates, eligibility, and final EMIs vary by lender, credit profile, loan-to-value rules, and future market conditions.

### True monthly ownership cost

Use this equation before deciding:

```text
True monthly housing cost = EMI
                          + society maintenance
                          + property tax provision
                          + repairs / appliance reserve
                          + home insurance provision
                          + parking / utilities difference
                          + commute or transport impact
```

### Personal affordability guardrail

Before finalizing, calculate these three ratios from your post-tax monthly income:

```text
Housing burden ratio = True monthly housing cost / monthly take-home income
EMI-to-savings ratio = EMI / amount left after essential costs
Safety ratio = (emergency fund remaining after purchase) / monthly essential expenses
```

A home is only affordable if it allows continued life: family needs, insurance, investing, medical contingencies, normal enjoyment, and the ability to change jobs without panic.

## 8. Property requirement brief

Create a one-page requirement sheet before browsing intensively.

### Essential requirements

- 2-BHK with clearly stated **RERA carpet area**, not only built-up or super built-up area.
- Liveable layout: usable bedrooms, adequate ventilation, kitchen space, storage, drying area, natural light.
- Lift access and low-friction entry for parents; check stairs, lift backup, and building access.
- Reliable water supply, electricity, sewage/drainage, and mobile/internet connectivity.
- Reasonable access to hospital, pharmacy, grocery, market, public transport, and railway/highway route as relevant.
- Safe approach road, practical parking arrangement if needed, and a functioning society or clear maintenance plan.
- Property type and possession status clearly understood: ready-to-move, under construction, resale, or MHADA allotment.

### Preferences to decide later

- Exact commute radius after job location is known.
- New construction versus resale.
- Gated society amenities versus lower monthly maintenance.
- Floor preference, balcony, parking type, view, clubhouse, gym, and other non-essential amenities.

### Red flags from the first visit

- Pressure to pay token immediately.
- Carpet area is unclear or different from brochure claim.
- No clear road access, frequent water-tanker dependence, visible seepage, poor ventilation, or unfinished common areas.
- Builder/seller refuses documents or says “all papers will come later.”
- The deal looks dramatically cheaper than comparable nearby homes without a clear, verified reason.

## 9. Search workflow

### Phase 0 — Prepare (now)

- Build personal cash-flow sheet and net-worth snapshot.
- List all assets, liabilities, SIPs, insurance, family obligations, and monthly essential expenses.
- Build emergency fund target.
- Create a digital folder for home documents and research.
- Define property brief and budget ceiling.
- Begin monitoring Pune/PCMC localities and MHADA official updates.

### Phase 1 — Become purchase-ready

- Improve career profile and apply for stronger jobs.
- Increase dedicated home-fund contribution after emergency-fund progress.
- Keep near-term home money appropriately low-risk as the purchase date approaches.
- Check CIBIL/credit report and correct errors early.
- Collect income and tax documents needed for a future loan.

### Phase 2 — Narrow locations

- Make 2–3 Pune/PCMC visits specifically for locality research.
- Visit each shortlisted micro-market on weekday morning, weekday evening, and weekend if possible.
- Test actual travel routes rather than trusting map times.
- Speak with residents, guards, shopkeepers, and society office staff.
- Record water, parking, traffic, noise, construction, safety, maintenance, and access conditions.

### Phase 3 — Evaluate actual homes

- View multiple comparable 2-BHKs before emotionally committing to one.
- Maintain a comparison sheet for each property.
- Request documents before a token payment.
- Get independent legal and technical review.
- Obtain loan sanction in principle and compare lender terms.

### Phase 4 — Commit carefully

- Negotiate total all-in price, not only base price.
- Pay only through traceable banking channels.
- Ensure the agreement accurately records all payments, timelines, inclusions, parking, possession, and consequences of default/delay.
- Do not rely on verbal assurances.

### Phase 5 — Close and move

- Complete registration correctly.
- Take possession only with documented checks.
- Transfer utilities and society records.
- Maintain a secure document archive.
- Keep a post-purchase repair and furnishing budget.

## 10. Legal and fraud-prevention checklist

> **Important:** Use an independent Maharashtra property lawyer. The builder’s or broker’s lawyer is not your independent verifier. This list helps you ask the right questions; it does not replace professional legal advice.

### A. Before paying any token

- [ ] Seller / developer identity verified; PAN and authority to sell are clear.
- [ ] Exact flat number, tower, floor, carpet area, parking, and boundaries identified in writing.
- [ ] Project checked on MahaRERA if applicable; promoter, registration, approvals, completion timeline, and complaints reviewed.
- [ ] No payment made because of “today only” pressure.
- [ ] Price breakdown obtained in writing: base price, floor rise, parking, GST if applicable, maintenance, corpus, club charges, legal/admin charges, registration/stamp duty estimate, and every other charge.
- [ ] Home loan pre-eligibility or sanction-in-principle checked before becoming emotionally committed.

### B. Title and ownership review

- [ ] Lawyer verifies the title chain / ownership history for the required period.
- [ ] Sale deed / conveyance deed / prior title documents reviewed.
- [ ] Encumbrance search and loan/mortgage status checked.
- [ ] If resale: society share certificate, transfer history, maintenance dues, property-tax status, and seller’s authority verified.
- [ ] If inherited property: succession, release, probate, legal-heir, and consent documents checked as applicable.
- [ ] If power of attorney is used: its validity and scope are independently verified.
- [ ] No unresolved claim, litigation, acquisition issue, or ownership dispute is ignored.

### C. Land, approvals, and construction

- [ ] Approved building plan and commencement certificate reviewed.
- [ ] RERA details match the actual project and unit.
- [ ] Occupancy Certificate (OC) / Completion Certificate (CC) confirmed for ready-to-move property, as applicable.
- [ ] Building use is residential and legally approved.
- [ ] Carpet area in agreement matches the RERA / approved disclosure.
- [ ] For under-construction property: stage of construction, possession date, delay clauses, and escrow/RERA compliance examined.
- [ ] No unauthorised floor, extension, terrace enclosure, or layout deviation is accepted casually.

### D. Society and practical checks

- [ ] Society registration status checked.
- [ ] Written confirmation of maintenance dues, sinking fund, and special levies obtained.
- [ ] Parking is clearly stated—open, covered, stilt, mechanical, or merely “assured.”
- [ ] Water source, tanker dependency, power backup, lift maintenance, security, garbage, drainage, and fire safety inspected.
- [ ] Physical inspection for seepage, cracks, plumbing, electrical points, dampness, ventilation, sunlight, noise, and mobile network completed.
- [ ] Actual usable carpet layout verified rather than relying on showroom model or brochure.

### E. Agreement and payment

- [ ] Independent lawyer reviews the agreement before signing.
- [ ] Agreement records flat, carpet area, parking, total cost, included fixtures, payment schedule, possession date, delay penalty, refund clause, and defect liability obligations.
- [ ] Every payment is banking-channel traceable and acknowledged by official receipt.
- [ ] No large cash component or unrecorded payment is accepted.
- [ ] No blank form, blank cheque, or incomplete document is signed.
- [ ] Registration is completed promptly; original documents and certified copies are secured.

### F. Possession

- [ ] OC / possession documents available as required.
- [ ] Snag list completed: doors, windows, tiles, plumbing, electrical points, switches, drainage, kitchen, bathrooms, balconies, walls, seepage, paint, and common areas.
- [ ] Meter readings, keys, access cards, parking allocation, and society handover documented.
- [ ] Utilities and society transfer records completed.

## 11. MHADA opportunity track

MHADA can be financially valuable, but it has scheme-specific eligibility, income-category rules, documents, deposits, lottery odds, possession timelines, and restrictions.

### Rules for using this track

- Use official MHADA channels as the source of truth: **mhada.gov.in** and **housing.mhada.gov.in**.
- Read the current scheme booklet fully before applying; do not rely only on videos, brokers, or headline prices.
- Apply only if eligible and if the specific location, carpet area, total cost, and possession condition suit family life.
- Maintain a ready folder of Aadhaar, PAN, Maharashtra domicile evidence, ITR/income documents, bank details, photograph, and reservation documents if applicable.
- Confirm whether the applicable income category is EWS/LIG/MIG/HIG and how household income is counted in the live scheme.
- Budget for EMD and understand refund timings; do not lock funds needed for emergencies.
- If allotted, still do the same location, all-in-cost, quality, and legal diligence before committing.

### Important question

With current compensation around ₹26 LPA, determine your actual eligibility carefully from the live scheme booklet. Many affordable categories have income caps; some HIG or developer-linked schemes may have different rules.

## 17. Anti-rogue rules

When I feel urgency, pressure, or fear of missing out, I will return to these rules:

1. **No house is worth a bad title.**
2. **No verbal promise is as valuable as a verified document.**
3. **No broker deadline overrides independent legal review.**
4. **The advertised flat price is not the total cost.**
5. **A low EMI can hide a very large total-interest burden.**
6. **A lower price does not compensate for poor water, bad access, unsafe construction, or an unliveable location.**
7. **Do not empty the emergency fund to feel closer to ownership.**
8. **Do not let family love become financial panic.**
9. **Compare several homes before committing to one.**
10. **The right home should create stability, not years of fear.**
